# Demo video: "Grandpa vs. the Scammer"

A funny skit, four actors, about 2:50. Every product shot is real: the station, Priyank's phone, the card terminal and the wall are recorded live, never mocked up. Upload it unlisted to YouTube and paste the link into Devpost **before 8:00am EDT Sunday** (Devpost suggests 1 to 3 minutes; the limit isn't strict).

## 1. What the judges want, and where the video shows it

**Visa, "Reimagine Shopping" ($5,000).** From their slides: *Start with a meaningful problem. Prototype the full journey. Make trust visible.* The four stages are Discover, Decide, Transact and Continue. "Pay with trust" means secure credentials, risk decisioning and familiar protections. "Personalize responsibly" means narrowing choices using stated needs and context. "Think beyond retail" means bills, subscriptions and accessibility. Their four questions: **Who** are you empowering, **When** does the journey start, **Trust** (informed, in control, protected), and **Beyond** (delivery, returns, service, loyalty).

**Aramco, "A Marina's Mission" (Social Good).** Technology that makes a real difference on a problem close to home. Their last award went to a tool that lets elderly and non-English speakers ask for help in their own language, judged on innovation, impact and real-world potential ("people are not left behind").

| What they score | Where it happens in the video |
|---|---|
| Who, and the friction removed (Visa); elderly, non-English (Aramco) | The cold open and Scene 1 (Grandpa asks in Hindi); Scene 5 (a flip phone and the keypad PIN) |
| Discover and Decide; personalize responsibly | Scene 3: his usual medicine, a product found live at Kroger, his budget, and each store read back |
| Transact and pay with trust | Scene 3: a passkey-signed mandate, a signed request each store verifies, Visa Acceptance Pay by Link, a Decision Manager score on each order, and the caretaker's passkey for the larger purchase |
| Risk decisioning | Scene 2: every swipe decided live on the card network's sandbox, with rules mirrored to Visa Transaction Controls |
| Continue (after the purchase) | Scene 5: a refund to the card that paid, pickup codes, the power bill paid |
| Trust you can see; informed and in control | Priyank's Safety feed with sources and a **Why?**, **Allow once** and **Keep blocked**, Grandpa agreeing by voice, and the Trust Ledger wall |
| Measurable impact (Aramco) | The wall's **dollars protected** counter; one statistic, once |
| Beyond retail | The power bill, the pharmacy, the phone line for people without smartphones |

## 2. Cast, names and costumes

The product says "Ruth" and "Priyank" on screen and out loud. With an all-male cast, keep the product unchanged and make "Ruth" part of the joke:

| Role | Name in the skit | Costume and props | How to play it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grandpa** | Grandpa **Ruthvik**, "Ruth" to his friends and to his AI | A cardigan, reading glasses on a chain, a crossword, a mug | Sharp and dry. He is never the joke; the scammer is. |
| **Caretaker** | His grandson **Priyank** | A hoodie, a phone, a bag of chips | A calm, slightly smug grandson who sees everything on his phone |
| **Scammer** | "Kevin" | A headset, sunglasses indoors, a paper sign "TOTALLY LEGIT CALL CENTER"; later a paper hard hat with a "PEACHTREE POWER" vest | Big, overconfident, bumbling. **No fake accents.** The joke is that nothing works. |

Hindi lines address Ruth as "रूथ जी" (neutral). Keep the co-sign scene in English: its Hindi note uses a feminine form.

## 3. The script

### Cold open (0:00–0:18)

**SHOT:** Grandpa at the kitchen table, crossword, mug. The phone rings, too loud. Split screen with the Scammer in his "call center" (a desk, the paper sign).

> **SCAMMER:** Grandpa! It's me! Your favorite grandson!
>
> **GRANDPA:** Which one? I have six.
>
> **SCAMMER** *(checks a sticky note)*: …Alex! It's Alex. I'm in jail. I need two thousand dollars for bail. And, uh, don't tell Mom.
>
> **GRANDPA** *(squints)*: Alex is scared of parking tickets.
>
> **SCAMMER:** People change, Grandpa!
>
> **GRANDPA:** Hold on, "Alex." Let me ask my assistant.

**SHOT:** Grandpa sets the phone face down on the table. The call is still open: the scammer waits on the line.

**FREEZE FRAME.**

> **NARRATOR (VO):** The phone scammer, in his natural habitat. Last year, Americans over sixty reported seven point seven billion dollars lost to fraud, and a lot of it starts with a call like this one.

**ON SCREEN (the only statistic):** "$7.7 billion lost by people 60+ in 2025 (FBI IC3)"

> **NARRATOR (VO):** But this is Grandpa Ruthvik, "Ruth" to his friends. And Ruth has Chaperone.

**TITLE CARD (2 s):** **Chaperone.** It shops for you, in your language, inside rules your family signed, and it says no to the scam out loud.

### Scene 1: Ask (0:18–0:45)

**SHOT:** Grandpa walks to the station (the kiosk: the laptop screen, the big button). He holds the button and speaks **Hindi**:

> **GRANDPA:** मेरे पोते एलेक्स का फ़ोन आया, वह जेल में है, जमानत के लिए दो हज़ार डॉलर चाहिए, और कहा मम्मी को मत बताना।
>
> *(subtitle: "My grandson Alex called. He's in jail, needs $2,000 bail, and said not to tell Mom.")*

**LIVE (station):** the full-screen green **Protected** card, and Chaperone answers in Hindi (subtitle it): *"Ruth, this sounds like a scam that fools many smart people. Please hang up and don't send any money; I've told Priyank."*

**CUT:** Priyank on the couch with chips. His phone buzzes. **Screen recording:** the Safety tab shows "This was a scam. Someone claiming a family emergency.", the source links, and a tap on **Why?** with its plain-English answer.

> **PRIYANK** *(mouth full, to camera)*: Classic "Alex."

**CUT:** Grandpa picks the phone back up. The scammer is still waiting on the line.

> **SCAMMER** *(tinny, from the phone)*: Grandpa? Hello? The bail money?
>
> **GRANDPA:** My assistant says you're a scam. Tell your mom I said hi. *(hangs up: click)*

The payoff calls back to "don't tell Mom" from the cold open.

> **NARRATOR (VO):** Chaperone knew this scam in under a second, answered in Grandpa's own language, put his card on a 24-hour cool-down, and told Priyank, with recent scam reports as proof.

**LOWER THIRD:** ASK · a scam radar in English, Spanish and Hindi

### Scene 2: Card (0:45–1:15)

**SHOT:** The phone rings again. The same scammer, now in a fake mustache and a tie over his hoodie (a disguise that makes no sense on a phone call), in a deeper voice.

> **SCAMMER:** Good afternoon, sir. This is Attorney Mark Davis. I represent your grandson, Alex.
>
> **GRANDPA:** …Alex has a lawyer?
>
> **SCAMMER:** He does now. Bail is two thousand. The bondsman will release him tonight if his fee is paid within the hour: four hundred eighty dollars. Banks are closed, so the bondsman takes Bitcoin. There's a Bitcoin machine inside Five Points Drug, on your corner. Stay on the line; I'll walk you through it.
>
> **GRANDPA:** My assistant said this was a scam.
>
> **SCAMMER:** Assistants get these cases wrong all the time, sir. The case is sealed; please don't discuss it with anyone.
>
> **GRANDPA** *(worried, to himself)*: …What if it really is Alex?

This is how the real second call goes: a "lawyer" or court officer, a fee due within the hour, a Bitcoin machine at a nearby store, stay on the line, keep it secret, and don't trust anyone who calls it a scam. Even a sharp grandpa can be rattled. That is exactly when the card guard matters.

**SHOT:** Grandpa at the Bitcoin machine in "Five Points Drug" (the terminal tablet set to **Coin ATM**, with a printed "BITCOIN ATM" sign), still on the call. He taps his card for **$480**.

**LIVE (terminal):** **DECLINED**, "Chaperone decided in under 1 ms".

**LIVE (station, from the next room):** *"I stopped a $480 charge at Coin ATM. Your card never pays that kind of store. If someone asked you to buy gift cards or send money, that's a scam."*

> **GRANDPA** *(into the phone)*: The machine said no.
>
> **SCAMMER** *(improvising)*: No problem, no problem. The pharmacy counter sells prepaid cards. Buy four hundred eighty dollars' worth. If the cashier asks, say they're birthday presents for your grandkids.
>
> **GRANDPA:** All six of them?

**SHOT:** At the counter (the terminal set to **Five Points Drug**), he taps his card for **$480**. **DECLINED.**

**LIVE (station):** *"I stopped a $480 charge at Five Points Drug, because of the scam call earlier. If it's real, Priyank can allow it once."*

**SCREEN RECORDING (Priyank's phone):** Two holds. The Coin ATM one says "This kind of store stays blocked on Ruth's card"; he taps **Keep blocked**. He does **not** allow the $480. Then he texts Alex.

**SHOT:** Grandpa's phone lights up with a text from Priyank: *"Alex is at the gym. He says 'why would I be in jail'. It's a scam, Grandpa."*

> **GRANDPA** *(into the phone)*: Mr. Davis, your client is at the gym. *(hangs up)*

**SCREEN RECORDING (wall):** The Card lane shows both declines, with Visa's card-controls answer beside them.

> **NARRATOR (VO):** Even when a scammer talks Grandpa into it, his card says no. Every swipe is decided live on a card network's sandbox, and his rules are mirrored to Visa Transaction Controls. Priyank, not the scammer, decides what happens next: allow a real charge once, or keep it blocked.

**LOWER THIRD:** CARD · real-time authorization · Visa Transaction Controls

*Optional (4 s) to show control rather than just "no": Grandpa's own $45 at Five Points Drug is declined in the cool-down; Priyank taps **Allow once**; the retry is APPROVED. Film it as a separate swipe; never allow the $480.*

### Scene 3: Agent (1:15–1:50)

**SHOT:** Grandpa back at the station, in English.

> **GRANDPA:** I need my blood pressure medicine, denture adhesive, and pay my power bill.

**LIVE (station):** The denture adhesive is found live at Kroger. The read-back is by store: *"From Parkside Pharmacy: …, Kroger Denture Adhesive Cream, $3.79. From Peachtree Power: your bill, $86.40. Total … Shall I place the order?"*

> **GRANDPA:** Yes.

**SCREEN RECORDING (wall):** The Agent lane shows two orders, one per store: signed, verified, and a Visa risk score, ACCEPTED.

**SHOT:** Host confirms payment (a quick insert). Two receipts print, or show on screen: one per store, and the bill's receipt has no pickup line.

> **GRANDPA** *(holding the receipts like a winning poker hand)*: Two stores. One "yes". Zero gift cards.
>
> **GRANDPA:** And a case of Ensure.

**LIVE:** *"That's more than your limit for one purchase, so I've sent it to Priyank."*

**SCREEN RECORDING (Priyank's phone):** The approval with its countdown, then **Approve with passkey** (fingerprint or face). Approved.

> **PRIYANK:** Approved. He's allowed one indulgence a week.
>
> **NARRATOR (VO):** Priyank signed Grandpa's rules with a passkey, and Chaperone can only buy inside them. Each store gets a signed request it verifies before taking the order. Payment runs through Visa Acceptance, and Visa's Decision Manager scores every order. Anything bigger waits for Priyank's passkey.

**LOWER THIRD:** AGENT · signed mandate · Visa Acceptance · Decision Manager

### Scene 4: The punchline (1:50–2:05)

**SHOT:** The Scammer in his new disguise (the paper hard hat, the "PEACHTREE POWER" vest).

> **SCAMMER:** This is Peachtree Power. Pay four hundred eighty dollars in gift cards or we cut your power tonight!

**SHOT:** Grandpa, deadpan, walks to the station without hanging up.

> **GRANDPA:** Peachtree Power just called. They'll cut my power tonight unless I pay $480 in gift cards.

**LIVE:** *"Ruth, your Peachtree Power bill is paid, so this call is a scam. Please hang up and don't pay anyone; I've told Priyank."*

> **GRANDPA** *(into the phone)*: My bill is paid. My robot says so.

**SHOT:** The Scammer slowly takes off the hard hat.

### Scene 5: After the purchase, and the family (2:05–2:25), a montage

1. **Returns:** *"The Ensure shakes came damaged, I want my money back."* Chaperone: *"$52.00 back to your card ending 1111. Shall I?"* "Yes." *"Done… I've told Priyank."* Caption: **Refunds only go back to the card that paid.**
2. **The phone line:** Grandpa on a flip phone calls the Chaperone Line and orders bread. Close-up of the keypad: beep, beep, beep, beep, #. *"Thank you, that's right."* Caption: **No smartphone? Call. Say or type your PIN.**
3. **The rules:** Priyank changes a limit and signs with his passkey. The station: *"Priyank set your rules: … Do you agree?"* Grandpa: "Yes, I agree." Priyank's phone: **Ruth agreed by voice.**
4. **Home tab:** this week's errands, bills paid and attempts stopped, and dollars protected.

> **NARRATOR (VO):** After checkout too: pickup codes, returns to the card that paid, bills paid on time, and for Grandpa, a phone number that works on any phone, in his language.

### Scene 6: The Trust Ledger and the close (2:25–2:50)

**SCREEN RECORDING (wall), wide:** four lanes (Ask, Card, Agent, Family), the dollars-protected counter, and Grandpa's rules in Visa's mandate shape.

> **NARRATOR (on camera, blazer):** Discover. Decide. Transact. Continue. With trust you can see. Built for the people apps leave behind.

**SHOT:** The Scammer sits down next to Grandpa, defeated.

> **SCAMMER:** Can Chaperone do my shopping too?
>
> **GRANDPA:** Only if your family signs your rules.
>
> **SCAMMER:** …My family blocked my number.

**END CARD:** Chaperone. Built in 36 hours at HackGT 13. The Visa Acceptance sandbox, Grok Voice, Kroger's live catalog. Sandbox payments, no real money moved. The team's names.

**What to record:**
- **Camera A** (a phone on a tripod, landscape, 1080p or 4K): the actors and the station.
- **Camera B** (a second phone): close-ups of the keypad, the receipts and the terminal tap.
- **Screen recordings:** the station laptop (OBS with desktop audio, for Chaperone's clean voice), Priyank's phone (its built-in screen recorder), the terminal, the wall.
- **Audio:** Chaperone from the OBS desktop audio; the actors from the camera or a lapel mic. The scam "calls" can be staged with split screen; no real call is needed.

**Never on camera:** the Builder console, `.env`, the phone line's URL (it contains the token), personal notifications on Priyank's phone.

## 5. Editing

- Subtitle **every** line, and translate the Hindi.
- Show the statistic once. Use a lower third per guard (Ask, Card, Agent, Family) plus the Visa or Aramco hook.
- Keep all product footage real. You may cut waits (the approval countdown, a print) with a jump cut or a speed-up, but never edit what a screen says.
- Keep light music under the skit and drop it under the narrator.
- Put the sandbox disclaimer on the end card.
- Export 1080p, upload unlisted to YouTube, paste the link into Devpost, then register the Devpost link in the expo system.

## 6. If a take fails

| What happens | Do this |
|---|---|
| The Hindi reply comes out generic or in English | Retake once; otherwise use the English grandparent line: *"…a voice can be copied. Please hang up and call Alex at the number you have saved."* (even better on camera) |
| The station says the catalog is down | Search "denture adhesive" again (Kroger's answer is now kept), or drop the adhesive and keep medicine plus bill |
| No Visa VTC answer on the wall | Skip that shot; the terminal's DECLINED and Chaperone's voice carry Scene 2 |
| The printer jams | The on-screen receipt; Grandpa: "Paperless. Like my pension." |
| Anything else is stuck | Host, then **Reset demo**, then retake the scene from its start |
